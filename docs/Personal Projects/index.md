# Personal Projects: Creating My Own Toolset

## I've always been fulfilled by optimizing and automating the processes of my daily life. My life is given meaning through intention, and optimization requires immense intention.

### In middle school, I began to label the internals of my backpack thoroughly so I'd never lose a pencil or a notebook.
### In high school, I created a script to determine my outfit everyday based on temperature, clothing intercompatability, laundry availability.
### In college, I began scheduling my work times based on an algorithm that maximized my learning by allowing me ample time for review and exploration.

## Now, I've put it all together in the Reactive Ambitious Manager or RAM.
# RAM is a self-hosted app I've built that automates my ambitious optimization goals.

## RAM's domain:
- My daily schedule and calender (synced with my family's Google Calender and my professional availability calender)
    - Academic assignments
    - Personal projects and learning time
    - Accounts for appointments and social commitments
    - Plans showers, shaves, and haircuts around outfit changes, workouts**\***, and formal events
- My daily outfit(s) with input from my calendar to determine formality and activity**\*** level.
- My diet and grocery list based on recipes I'd like to make and my workout**\*** plans.

**\***See [RUN:FIT](/docs/Athletics/) plugin for exercise-related features.

## RAM has managed these parts of my life for me based on my own algoritms and priorities since July 2025. It has support for a few of my modalities (full-time research, in-semester, and winter break)
I'm currently working to expand functionality so it's optimization toolset can be used by those along all paths. When I feel that it is versatile enough to be useful to others, I'll be posting it on GitHub as an opensource project. Currently, its respository is hosted privately by [Harold](/docs/Personal Projects/#harold-my-highschool-computer-repurposed-for-personal-remote-computing).

# Other Personal Projects

## Harold, my highschool computer repurposed for personal remote computing
Harold runs a minimal Debian Server Linux installation.
It uses Docker Compose managed by Portainer to serve:
- **Music.** I don't believe the digital age of music has to come at the expense of a personally curated listening experience that rewards advertisement salesmen over artists. I spend **less** money per year on compared to Spotify Premion by purchasing albums on Bandcamp and other direct sales platforms. I use a Tailscale Funneled HTTPS address to securely serve my library to my family's devices.
    Stack: Lidarr for database, Navidrome for server, Feishin for Client.
- **Books.** I mostly read paper books from my university's library, but sometimes, I want to support an author or read a unaccessible book, so I store my collection on Harold and serve them to my Kindle device through Calibre and CalibreWeb.
- **Storage.** After my highschool GSuite account expired, I moved all of my document and data storage onto Harold. I maintained Google Drive functionality through a combination of NextCloud for data that stays in my cloud long term and Resilio for real time document and file syncing.
- **Pictures.** When I wanted to switch from Google Images to another image hosting site, Google would only let me download pictures one at a time or all at once without any metadata. Once I repaired my metadata, I decided no one would keep my memories ransom again, so I store my pictures on Harold with Immich, a suprisingly sophisticated, GPU accelerated image server with ML tagging just like the big boys.
- **Password Manager.** I use VaultWarden, which is compatible with BitWarden Clients.
