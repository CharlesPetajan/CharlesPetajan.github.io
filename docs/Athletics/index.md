## I apply the same problem solving philosophy to my athletic goals. Here is my framework to maximize my fulfillment out of this pursuit.

### What do you output?
Exercising, learning, and drilling is the most important factor in sports, obviously, but how do you get the most enjoyment and progress out of your time?
### What do you input?
Diet and comsumption habits affect performance, but they also provide personal fulfillment themselves, so how do you balance priorities?
### Executive Overhead
Some people love to meticulously schedule their days with colored highlighters, but I want to delegate as much "executive overhead" as possible. I love solving problems once, but computers can do the rest after that.

## **Putting it all together: RLM:FIT, a division of [RLM (Responsive Life Management)](/docs/Personal%20Projects/)**

### RLM:FIT uses algorithms I've developed to plan workouts, meet short and medium term goals, and push users towards progressive overload.
{: .no_toc}
- Progressive Overload is the generally accepted approach for growth across all training intensities.
- For my endurance pursuits, low-intensity high-volume training should be 80% of time and high-intensity is 20%.
    - low-intensity high-volume training should be ~75% of time. Progressive overload targets *longer* training sessions.
    - high-intensity should be ~25%. Progressive overload targets *faster* training sessions

### RLM:FIT fits polynomial "Progressive Overload" curves based on medium term goals set in my [RLM Android App and WebUI](/docs/Personal%20Projects/). These curves determine the targeted session length or speed when RLM:FIT tries to schedule a workout that fits into my [RLM Schedule](/docs/Personal%20Projects/)
{: .no_toc}
### RLM:FIT adjusts suggested weekly workout volume based on whether session targets were met that week. It then adjusts curves to stay on pace for medium term goals.
{: .no_toc}
- Volume not met? Lower pace of low-intesity workouts
- Pace not met? Lower volume of high-intensity workouts

### RLM:FIT is the coach that pushes you to push yourself and helps you get back when you slip
{: .no_toc}

# Tri The Illini Triathlon 2024
## In 2024, I was selected by my teammates in The University of Illinois Triathlon Club to organize and direct our annual triathlon event alongside my co-director, Charlie Doubleday.
{: .no_toc}
### My responsibilities:
- Coordinating with university, local, and county officials to secure the necessary roads, sidewalks, and facilities.
- Planning and executing the event, including coordinating our team of over 40 volunteers.
- Advertising to and managing the extensive needs of our 500+ participants.
- Ensuring that the event turns a profit for the organization. We took away over $12k while keeping the event accessible to students and community members.

### What I've taken away: 
- Grace and poise are very valuable in situations where perfection is impossible.
- Planning for every possible mistake requires focus and intention.
- At 4 AM, feeding people breakfast is the best thing you can do for them.
